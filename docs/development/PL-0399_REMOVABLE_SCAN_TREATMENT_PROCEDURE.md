# PL-0399 — Removable scanning treatment decision procedure

## Default: do not apply a surface treatment

First try non-contact alternatives: stable diffuse illumination and exposure, a contrasting matte background, additional viewpoints, careful camera angle changes, and (for suitable glossy opaque surfaces) the optional PL-0398 cross-polarized image set. Keep transparent/translucent packages as a distinct optical problem. If the required surface remains unobservable, record the affected region and reconstruction limitation rather than altering a valuable, finished, printed, filled, or production package.

A scanning spray or other temporary coating changes the object's surface while it is present. A product marketed as vanishing or sublimating is not automatically compatible with a particular resin, molded-in additives, coating, print, label adhesive, seal, or product residue; it does not establish food-contact suitability or zero residue. AESUB's current Blue SDS (revision 2025-01-27) advises against use on products that contact food and against private/household use; it classifies that product as an extremely flammable aerosol, pressurized container, a single-exposure narcotic-effects hazard, and an aquatic chronic hazard. The manufacturer's 2022 Blue technical sheet and current product page give an approximately 8–15 μm coating range, state specific-use material compatibility is not guaranteed, and state complete sublimation is not guaranteed. These are product-specific statements, not universal scan-spray properties. No product is selected for PackLab. The SDS's private/household restriction is recorded as written; a noncommercial software purpose does not by itself establish whether a future factory or design-office application is household/private or permitted. Since no such workplace is currently established, any future use requires review of the exact regional SDS, supplier guidance, and actual site/use by responsible EHS and package-owner roles before application. Do not use in a household/private setting when the current SDS advises against it. The manufacturer provides current product SDS and technical-sheet downloads; obtain exact product and regional documents before any contemplated use.

## Decision gates — every gate must pass

1. **Need:** document why non-contact lighting/viewpoint alternatives cannot meet the capture need and identify the specific surfaces that require treatment.
2. **Object authority and use:** identify package resin and surface stack as far as available (including pigment, coating, print/ink, label, adhesive, seals, contents/residue and intended use). Obtain explicit authorization from the responsible package owner. If intended use is unknown, the package is filled, it may contact food, medicine, cosmetics or other regulated contents, or it is a saleable/irreplaceable reference, do not treat it. Never apply scan chemicals inside a container.
3. **Product evidence:** record the exact product, lot, current technical data sheet, current region-specific safety data sheet, claimed coating thickness, working/sublimation time, application instructions, compatibility limitations, product-specific advised-against uses, and product-manufacturer confirmation for the specific surface/use when necessary. If the document is unavailable, ambiguous or says compatibility is not guaranteed without the required coupon check, do not treat the real object. In particular, the current AESUB Blue SDS advises against private/household use. Have the supplier/EHS determine whether the actual future workplace and intended use fall within that restriction; noncommercial PackLab status alone does not decide it. No workplace or product is currently selected.
4. **Site safety:** obtain local workplace EHS authorization. Follow that exact SDS for ventilation, ignition controls, storage, PPE, exposure response and disposal. Use an appropriate designated spray area with containment; protect camera, turntable, lights, room and other people from aerosol deposition. Do not spray near flame, ignition sources, hot equipment or occupied process zones. If the required ventilation, PPE, fire controls or containment are unavailable, stop. This procedure is not a substitute for a safety assessment or local legal review.
5. **Stop, incident response and disposition plan:** before applying anything, write down product/site-specific stop criteria, spill or overspray response, response to unexpected surface change/exposure, containment, manufacturer-approved cleanup or an explicit no-cleanup/quarantine route, waste handling, and final sample disposition. Have the material owner and EHS approve it. Do not start if any credible outcome has no authorized response or disposition.
6. **Sacrificial compatibility test:** on a disposable coupon matching the actual resin, colorant, coating, print, adhesive and cure/age state, use the exact product and instructions. Inspect before, during and after the full stated disappearance period for softening/swelling, crazing, color/gloss change, tack, lifting, ink transfer, residue and effects of any permitted cleanup. A generic HDPE/PET/PP coupon does not establish compatibility with a finished package surface stack. If a true equivalent coupon is unavailable or any change is observed, do not treat the production sample.
7. **Measurement validity:** decide whether the coating's stated/observed layer thickness and uneven deposition can bias the measurements that matter. For dimension-reference work, remain untreated unless coating thickness, uniformity and dimensional correction are independently measured and validated on the same process. Do not treat a spray-matted geometry as uncoated ground truth. Preserve original untreated captures and independent reference dimensions first.

## If all gates later pass for an authorized disposable or noncritical sample

- Capture and retain the untreated baseline photographs and reference measurements first. Record object/sample identity in the internal system, material-stack evidence, intended use, approving roles, product name/lot, SDS/TDS revision/date, room conditions and measurement purpose.
- Follow the exact manufacturer directions and site EHS controls. Keep only the manufacturer's recommended application amount and standoff; no improvised overcoating or alternate chemistry. Record start time, applied zones, coating/layer instructions and each scan time. Do not transfer generic vendor application distances or working times between products.
- Keep treated and untreated image sets and reconstruction outputs separate. Tag every treated image/model with product and lot, approximate application time, surfaces treated, and treatment caveat. A measured mesh includes the coating surface while it is present unless a validated correction removes that offset.
- Allow only the exact product's documented disappearance process under approved site conditions. Inspect the whole part under the documented light and surface condition; elapsed time alone is not proof that the coating is gone. Do not wipe, wash, heat, blow with a fan or apply solvent unless the product instructions and the material owner/EHS explicitly approve that method. If residue, tack, appearance change or uncertainty remains, execute the preapproved stop/incident-response/disposition plan; quarantine the sample and do not return it to contents-contact or production service.
- Log outcome as a specific sample/product/process trial. A successful coupon is not proof for another resin, colorant, label, print, closure, product residue, or production lot.
